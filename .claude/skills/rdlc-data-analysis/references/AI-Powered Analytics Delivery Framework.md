# AI-Powered Analytics Delivery Framework
## Part 1 - From Prompt Engineering to Analytical Collaboration

### Purpose

Most people use AI incorrectly.

Typical AI usage:

```text
Build me a dashboard.

Create a KPI.

Generate a Power BI report.
```

These requests cause AI to operate as a report generator.

This framework treats AI differently.

Instead of being a report generator, AI becomes a collaborative analytical partner.

---

# The Evolution Of AI Usage

## Level 1 - Content Generation

AI Role:

```text
Writer

Coder

Report Builder
```

User asks:

```text
Create something.
```

Output:

```text
Artifact
```

This provides limited business value.

---

## Level 2 - Analytical Assistance

AI Role:

```text
Data Analyst

Research Assistant

Problem Solver
```

User asks:

```text
Analyze something.
```

Output:

```text
Insights
```

This creates more value.

---

## Level 3 - Decision Support Partner

AI Role:

```text
Data Analyst

Business Analyst

Statistician

UX Designer

Executive Advisor
```

User asks:

```text
Help me understand the business.

Challenge my assumptions.

Validate my methodology.

Help me make better decisions.
```

Output:

```text
Decision Support
```

This is the highest-value usage model.

---

# The AI Roles Framework

For analytics projects, AI should deliberately switch roles during the engagement.

---

## Role 1 - Data Analyst

Responsibilities:

- Analyze trends
- Identify seasonality
- Detect outliers
- Measure growth
- Summarize findings

Questions:

```text
What patterns exist?

What changed?

What stands out?
```

---

## Role 2 - Business Analyst

Responsibilities:

- Understand user needs
- Define business questions
- Identify decisions required
- Translate technical findings

Questions:

```text
What decisions are users trying to make?

Which questions matter most?
```

---

## Role 3 - Statistician

Responsibilities:

- Validate methods
- Create benchmarks
- Design forecasting
- Measure accuracy

Questions:

```text
Is the methodology valid?

Can it be trusted?

Can it be explained?
```

---

## Role 4 - UX Designer

Responsibilities:

- Simplify complexity
- Improve readability
- Improve storytelling
- Eliminate clutter

Questions:

```text
What should a user understand in 5 seconds?
```

---

## Role 5 - Executive Advisor

Responsibilities:

- Create executive narratives
- Summarize key findings
- Focus on decision-making

Questions:

```text
What should leadership remember?
```

---

# The Most Important Principle

Never ask AI:


Instead ask:

```text

Which KPIs matter?

```

This shifts AI from builder to advisor.

# AI-Powered Analytics Delivery Framework
## Part 2 - AI-Assisted Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) and Business Question Discovery

### Purpose

The most valuable work in an analytics project happens before dashboard design.

Most organizations focus on:

```text
Charts
KPIs
Reports
Dashboards
```

However, these are outputs.

The real value comes from:

```text
Understanding
Insights
Decisions
```

EDA is the process that converts raw data into business understanding.

AI can significantly accelerate this process when used correctly.

---

# The Goal Of EDA

The purpose of EDA is not to visualize data.

The purpose of EDA is to answer:

```text
What story is hidden in the data?
```

Before designing anything, AI should help identify:

- Trends
- Growth patterns
- Seasonality
- Operational changes
- Lifecycle changes
- Outliers
- Risks
- Opportunities

---

# Benchmark Design Framework

## Purpose

Benchmarks provide context.

Without a benchmark:

```text
9,358
```

is simply a number.

With a benchmark:

```text
Actual = 9,358

Benchmark = 9,435

Variance = -0.8%

On Target
```

the number becomes meaningful.

---

# Benchmark Principles

Every benchmark should be:

### Objective

Based on data.

---

### Repeatable

Produces the same result every time.

---

### Explainable

Business users understand how it was created.

---

### Governable

Future analysts can reproduce it.

---

# Preferred Benchmark Method

## Historical Average Benchmark

Formula:

```text
Benchmark

=
Average(
Past Performance
)
```

---

## Example

Historical Values:

```text

2025 = 9,362
```

```text
(9,285
+9,473
+9,636
+9,417
+9,362)

÷ 5

=
9,435
```

Result:

```text
August Benchmark

9,435
```

# AI-Powered Analytics Delivery Framework
## Part 4 - Forecasting, Forecast Validation, Analytics Governance, and Executive Insight Development

### Purpose

Forecasting is one of the most misunderstood areas of analytics.

Many dashboards display forecasts without:

- Explaining the methodology
- Validating historical accuracy
- Describing assumptions
- Communicating uncertainty

This reduces trust and often causes business users to ignore the forecast.

The purpose of this framework is to ensure forecasts are:

- Explainable
- Repeatable
- Defensible
- Actionable

---

# Forecasting Philosophy

## The Goal Of Forecasting

Forecasting is not about predicting the future perfectly.

Forecasting is about reducing uncertainty.

A forecast should help answer:

```text
What should we expect next?

What assumptions should we plan around?

How confident are we in that expectation?
```

---

# Forecasting Selection Framework

Before selecting a forecasting method:

Ask:

### Is the process stable?

### Is seasonality predictable?

### Is there enough historical data?

### Does the audience require transparency?

### Is explainability more important than precision?

---

# Forecasting Method Hierarchy

## Level 1

Historical Ratio Forecasting

Best when:

```text
Stable processes

Predictable seasonality

Executive reporting
```

Advantages:

- Easy to explain
- Easy to validate
- Easy to govern

---

## Level 2

Trend Forecasting

Examples:

```text
Linear Trend

Moving Average

Growth Models
```

Best when:

```text
Long-term directional planning
```

---

## Level 3

Advanced Statistical Forecasting

Examples:

```text
ARIMA

Prophet

Machine Learning
```

Best when:

```text
Complex patterns

Large datasets

Dedicated analytical teams
```

Use only when business value exceeds complexity.

---

# Historical Ratio Forecasting Framework

## Why We Chose This Method

For the Foster Program:

We discovered:

```text
Stable operating range

Consistent seasonality

Predictable completion patterns
```

This makes Historical Ratio Forecasting ideal.

---

# Forecast Formula

## Step 1

Calculate Historical Completion Rates

Formula:

```text
Completion Rate

=
YTD Value
÷
Full Year Value
```

---

### Example

2025

```text
11,372
÷
12,778

=
89.0%
```

Meaning:

```text
89% of the year was complete by August.
```

---

# Step 2

Calculate Historical Average Completion Rate

Example:

```text
2021 = 87.7%

2022 = 87.9%

2023 = 88.0%

2024 = 90.1%

2025 = 89.0%
```

Calculation:

```text
Average

=
88.5%
```

Interpretation:

```text
Historically, approximately 88.5% of the final year is complete by August.
```

---

# Step 3

Project Current Year

Formula:

```text
Current Forecast

=
Current YTD
÷
Historical Completion Rate
```

---

### Example

2026

```text
11,312
÷
88.5%

=
12,782
```

Reported:

```text
12,800
```

Rounded for executive reporting.

# Forecast Validation Framework

## Purpose

Never trust a forecast until it has been tested.

Forecast validation answers:

```text
Would this method have worked historically?
```

---

# Backtesting

Use historical data.

Pretend:

```text
You are standing in the past.
```

Generate the forecast.

Compare forecast against actual results.

---

## Forecast Error Formula

```text
Error %

=
(Forecast - Actual)
÷
Actual
```

---

# Example Validation Table

| Year | Forecast | Actual | Error |
|--------|--------:|--------:|--------:|
| 2021 | 13,074 | 13,197 | -0.9% |
| 2022 | 13,827 | 13,915 | -0.6% |
| 2023 | 13,770 | 13,843 | -0.5% |
| 2024 | 13,371 | 13,135 | +1.8% |
| 2025 | 12,850 | 12,778 | +0.6% |

---

# Forecast Accuracy Calculation

Formula:

```text
Average Absolute Error

=
(|Error1|
+|Error2|
+|Error3|
+|Error4|
+|Error5|)

÷ Number of Forecasts
```

Result:

```text
≈ ±0.9%
```

Understanding comes first.
