About the status pills above BC SPCA's brand palette has no built-in red/yellow/green alert colours, so these pills use the closest brand-meaning match instead: Teal ("Community & Belonging," calm) for On Target, Mustard (closest warm tone, though its real brand meaning is "Celebration," not caution) for Monitor, and Berry ("Urgency") for Action Required. Q1. How Has Our Foster Volunteer Pool Evolved Over Time?

Illustrative trend: the Active pool grew through an early recruitment phase, then settled into a stable range — Active Fosters Over 1 Year (KPI 1) has grown as a share of that pool, suggesting the pool is maturing, not just turning over.
Takeaway: a maturing pool is a different story than a merely stable headcount — KPI 1/3 is what tells them apart.

Q2. Are We Losing Volunteers Faster Than We're Gaining Them?

Illustrative trend: New Foster Created (KPI 6) and Fosters Archived (KPI 4) have tracked closely, keeping the Archived–to–New Ratio (KPI 7) near 100% — recent periods show it edging past 100%, meaning losses are slightly outpacing new recruits.
Takeaway: this is the simple growth-vs-loss read — worth checking before escalating to the true attrition rate (KPI 8).

Q3. Is Our Volunteer Capacity Related to Animal-Side Demand?

Illustrative: Active Fosters (KPI 2) and % Animals in Foster (ShelterBuddy) appear to move together across this period (Correlation, Analysis Design Layer 3). This shows a relationship worth investigating, not a confirmed cause in either direction — and ShelterBuddy's own "% Animals in Foster" measure is itself still unconfirmed elsewhere in this project.
Takeaway: the first concrete statistical link between the volunteer side and the animal side — see the companion Analysis Design document for the full method and caveats.

Q4. Are We Losing People Early, or After Years?

Illustrative: using the Time With Us Before Leaving dimension (Dimension 12), exits cluster more in the "under 1 year" band than the "2+ year" band in this placeholder data — a pattern that would point toward onboarding/matching, not long-tenure burnout, if it held in real data.
Takeaway: this is exactly the breakdown a flat Archived count (KPI 4 alone) could never show.

Why a plain year-over-year comparison, not a monthly benchmark Unlike the animal side — which has a confirmed June–October seasonal peak — it is not yet confirmed whether Better Impact volunteer recruitment or archiving follows any seasonal pattern. Until that's established, comparing a single month against a multi-year seasonal average would risk presenting ordinary noise as if it were a real pattern. This page instead compares this year's running numbers to the same months last year — the simpler, safer read until seasonality (if any) is confirmed.

How Is This Year's Pool Tracking So Far?

Illustrative: Active Fosters (KPI 2), running month by month, compared to the same months last year. The current-year line sits consistently above last year's — the pool is larger at every point so far this year.
Takeaway: a larger pool this year is a separate fact from whether it's being maintained well — see the next panel for the flow in and out of it.

Are New Joins Keeping Pace With Archiving This Year?

Illustrative: New Foster Created (KPI 6) vs. Fosters Archived (KPI 4), month by month this year. In several recent months Archived has run ahead of New Foster Created — consistent with the YTD Archived–to–New Ratio (KPI 7) crossing above 100%.
Takeaway: this is the same simple growth-vs-loss read as the history page's Q2, just scoped to this year's running months.
