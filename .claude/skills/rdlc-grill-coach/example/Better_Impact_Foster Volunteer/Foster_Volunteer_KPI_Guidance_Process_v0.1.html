Guiding a Data Owner from a Rough KPI to a Governed Definition

A reusable, 6-step method — worked through using Lindsay's "Retention" proposal, but intended for any Data Owner's first-draft metric.

Internal Process Guidev0.1
Why this existsThis is the Decision-First framework applied concretely — not abstractly described — to one real metric, so the method is reusable the next time a Data Owner brings a rough first draft.

This is the method to use whenever a Data Owner hands us a rough, first-draft metric — not just for Lindsay's Retention proposal. The point isn't to correct them; it's to guide them, using their own words and their own data, to a definition that actually serves the decision they have in mind.

Treat their input as a valid signal of intent, not a flawed answer to correct. A rough KPI draft — "Retention = # active >1yr" — tells us what the Data Owner cares about, even if the formula itself can't carry that weight. Open by taking the concern seriously, not by pointing out what's wrong with the formula.
Ask what decision it's meant to support, in their own words. This is the one question that determines everything downstream — don't infer it, ask it directly and record their actual answer.
"If this number went up or down next quarter, what would you actually do differently?"
Stress-test their literal definition against one concrete, worked scenario — not an abstract objection. Show them a specific case, not a theory, so they see the gap themselves instead of being told about it.
"Volunteer A fostered once in March 2023, never got archived. Today, under your definition, they count as a retained >1yr member. Does that match what you meant?"
Once they agree the gap is real, show them why: the metric is bundling separable concepts. A single count is usually doing two or three jobs at once (e.g. status, tenure, utilization) that need to be tracked separately to answer the real decision from Step 2.
Propose a small number of named candidate definitions, each explicitly tied back to their Step 2 answer. Not a long menu — 2–3 concrete, named options, each one tested against "does this actually tell you what to do differently."
Confirmation gate. Nothing is settled until the Data Owner picks and explicitly confirms a definition — same rule as every other RDLC artifact in this project. A mid-discussion "sounds good" is not confirmation.
