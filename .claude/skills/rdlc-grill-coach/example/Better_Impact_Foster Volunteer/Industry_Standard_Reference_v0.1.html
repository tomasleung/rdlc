Headline finding, stated plainly There is no established industry-standard formula for foster volunteer retention or utilization — not from Shelter Animals Count, not from Maddie's Fund, not from Best Friends. What exists instead is strong, real behavioral research into why caregivers leave and what helps retain them, plus one animal-side (not volunteer-side) utilization metric. Treat everything below as context and validation for our approach, not a formula to adopt wholesale — BC SPCA is defining genuinely new ground on the volunteer-metrics side.

01Shelter Animals Count — animal-side only, no volunteer metric

Shelter Animals Count (SAC) — Animal Welfare Glossary: Calculations

shelteranimalscount.org/animal-welfare-glossary/calculations

SAC is the closest thing to a national standards body for shelter statistics. It defines foster population counts (animals in foster care on the first and last day of the month) and explicitly excludes foster/trial placements from its Adoption definition. It does not define any volunteer-side retention, tenure, or utilization metric.

What this confirmsOur instinct to keep the animal-side measure (Foster Utilization Rate's ShelterBuddy-anchored cut) and the volunteer-side measure (Member Tenure, Engaged Core Fosters) conceptually separate is consistent with how the sector's own standards body treats them — as different categories of thing, not one blended metric.

02Foster Utilization Ratio (FUR) — a real, peer-reviewed metric, but animal-side

Emergency Fostering of Dogs During COVID-19 — Frontiers in Veterinary Science, 2022

frontiersin.org/journals/veterinary-science/articles/10.3389/fvets.2022.862590 · PMC9093711

Formula: FUR = (dogs/puppies in foster) ÷ (dogs/puppies in foster + dogs/puppies still in shelter), computed daily, averaged monthly. Designed specifically to compare foster usage across shelters of different sizes.

Limitation the authors themselves note: FUR shows how much a shelter uses foster homes, but not why the share changes.

What this confirmsThis is structurally the same family as Phase 1's "% Animals in Foster" and CATU's "% Animals in Care Fostered" — both already built, both already peer-reviewed-adjacent in structure. It does not validate a volunteer-side utilization metric; it's animal-population-side, same as SAC's framing above.

03Maddie's Fund — Understanding Turnover of Foster Caregivers (the report you flagged)

Understanding Turnover of Foster Caregivers: Exploring the Reasons and Potential Solutions

maddiesfund.org/assets/grants/final-foster-turnover-report.pdf · 4,800+ surveyed/interviewed (869 ex-fosters, 739 taking a break, 2,788 current fosters)

This is real behavioral research, not a metrics standard — but it's the most directly relevant thing found, because it independently validates the segmentation approach already proposed.

A real, validated caregiver-type taxonomy

The study categorizes caregivers by level of service — almost exactly the axis our Status × Tenure × Utilization table already uses:

One-time"Fostering now but don't plan on fostering in the future"
Seasonal"Fostered at certain times of year, like summer, but not others"
Every-once-in-a-whileIntermittent, not regular (~39% of the current-foster sample)
FrequentAlmost continuous or continuous (~31% of the current-foster sample)
What this confirmsIndustry research independently landed on a "how much do they actually do" segmentation almost identical in spirit to our Foster Utilization Rate / Engaged Core Fosters split — this isn't a BC SPCA-invented framework, it mirrors what a 4,800-person study found useful for the same population.

Top 5 reasons for voluntary turnover

#Reason
1Needing time to focus solely on one's own pets
2Adopting too many of the animals themselves ("too many foster fails")
3Schedule not allowing for foster caregiving
4Personal issues (age/health, caregiving for loved ones, divorce, etc.)
5Living situation not allowing for foster caregiving
What this means for KPI interpretationMost top reasons for leaving are personal/circumstantial, not organizational failure. This directly shapes the Prohibited Conclusion language for Member Tenure/Engaged Core Fosters: a volunteer moving to Archived should not be read as "we failed them" by default — that's an unsupported causal leap the data alone can't make, consistent with this project's standing interpretation-boundary discipline.

What actually helps retention (the "buffers")

  • Feeling supported by the organization
  • Feeling that one's input matters / is listened to
  • Feeling appreciated
  • Training — especially basic handling/behavioral issues (notably under-delivered: 20–35% of ex-fosters, 15–25% of those taking a break, and 13–20% of current fosters reported receiving no training at all, and lack of training was negatively related to continuing)
  • Improved communication
  • The "foster team" model — over 80% of respondents agreed/strongly agreed this concept could work well
What this means for future workIf BC SPCA later wants to go beyond the quantitative KPIs into a qualitative "why are we losing fosters" investigation, this report's validated question set (training perception, feeling supported/heard/appreciated) is a ready-made, tested starting point — not something to design from scratch.

One honest limitation of this document

This report gives no single "retention rate %" figure usable as a sector benchmark/target — it's reasons-and-buffers research, not a KPI definitions catalog. Don't cite a number from this source as "the industry benchmark" because none is given in that form.

04What this means for our KPI design, overall

  • Keep animal-side and volunteer-side measures separate — confirmed consistent with how SAC and the FUR literature both treat them, not a BC SPCA-specific choice.
  • The Status × Tenure × Utilization segmentation is validated in spirit, not invented in a vacuum — independent research landed on a near-identical "level of service" framework.
  • No external benchmark % exists to target or compare against — any Fixed Target set for Engaged Core Rate % or Foster Utilization Rate will be BC SPCA's own call, grounded in its own baseline, not an industry number. Flag this to the Data Owner before any threshold gets set.
  • The "<25% of caregivers take multiple placements" finding (from the separate COVID emergency-fostering study referenced in the research turn before this document) is the closest thing to a sector data point for our Foster Utilization Rate's likely real-world range — useful context, not a target.
