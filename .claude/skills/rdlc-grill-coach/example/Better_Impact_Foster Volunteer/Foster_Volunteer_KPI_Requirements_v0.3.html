01  What Better Impact actually tracks

Every measure below is built from a volunteer's status in Better Impact. Here's the lifecycle, left to right:

Applicant
Just applied, not yet reviewed.
→
In Process
Being reviewed/onboarded.
→
Active / Accepted
Approved — a real foster volunteer.
→
Inactive
Temporary pause — may come back.
Archived
Permanent exit.
Read this before the measures below — everything after this point assumes the following, and none of it is confirmed yet
  • That the five statuses above match BC SPCA's actual configured Better Impact account — not just the general product.
  • That "Active/Accepted Date" and "Archived Date" are real, dedicated date fields we can actually pull — not something we'd have to approximate another way.
  • That if a volunteer goes Inactive → Active/Accepted again, we have no way to tell — the date field likely just shows their most recent change, silently losing the earlier one.
  • That if a volunteer is un-Archived (reinstated), the same problem applies — we wouldn't be able to tell that happened either.
If any of these turn out to be wrong, several of the measures below need to be rebuilt, not just re-run — so please treat the Questions for the Better Impact Admin section at the end as the real priority, not an afterthought.

02  The measures

Our default preference Wherever possible, we use Better Impact alone. Of the 10 measures below, 8 need only Better Impact. Only the Engagement pair (rows 9–10) requires joining to ShelterBuddy, because Better Impact has no field for which specific animal a volunteer fostered — that fact lives only in ShelterBuddy.
KeyPriorityAnalysis CategoryKPIDefinitionSource System
1P1Retention Active Fosters Over 1 Year How many volunteers currently marked Active/Accepted have held that status for more than one year? Better Impact
2P1Retention Active Fosters How many volunteers are currently marked Active/Accepted, regardless of how long they've held that status? Better Impact
3P1Retention % Active Fosters Over 1 Year Of all volunteers currently marked Active/Accepted (2), what percentage have held that status for more than a year (1)? Better Impact
4P1Retention Fosters Archived How many volunteers were moved to Archived status during the period (year, quarter, or month)? Better Impact
5P1Retention Not Yet Archived at Start of Period How many volunteers had already become Active/Accepted, and had not yet been moved to Archived, as of the start of that same period? This includes anyone currently Inactive — a temporary pause doesn't remove someone from this count, only Archived does. Better Impact
6P1Retention New Foster Created How many new volunteer records were first created (Applicant) during the period? Better Impact
7P1Retention Archived–to–New Ratio Archived during the period (4), divided by New volunteers who joined during that same period (6). A simple loss-versus-growth comparison — not the same thing as row 8, which measures loss against the people we already had, not the people who just joined. Better Impact
8P1Retention % Fosters Archived Of the volunteers we already had at the start of the period (5), what percentage were moved to Archived by the end of it (4)? Better Impact
9P2Engagement Active Fosters With a Recent Foster Placement How many currently Active/Accepted volunteers have taken an animal into foster care within the last 90 days? Better Impact + ShelterBuddy
10P2Engagement % Active Fosters With a Recent Foster Placement Of all currently Active/Accepted volunteers (2), what percentage have taken an animal into foster care in the last 90 days (9)? Better Impact + ShelterBuddy

Rows 7 and 8 use the exact same numerator (1 Archived) but different denominators (2 New vs. 3 Not Yet Archived) — giving 50% and 33.3%, two genuinely different numbers, not the same ratio restated. That's why they're kept as two separate, named measures rather than one "Attrition %."

Also worth noting: in this example, Sarah's break-and-return happened to land before Jan 1, 2025, so row 5 still counts her correctly. If her return had instead happened during 2025, she could have been wrongly left out of row 5 for that year — the assumption in Section 01 isn't theoretical, this is exactly where it would bite.

04  Dimensions

What's a "dimension"? It's a filtering/grouping term, not a number on its own. Instead of just knowing "12 Active Fosters," a dimension lets you break that 12 down further — for example, by how long each of them has been active. You don't read a dimension by itself; you use it to filter or group one of the measures in Section 02.
KeyPriorityAnalysis CategoryDimensionDefinitionSource System
11P2Retention Time as an Active Foster Groups volunteers currently marked Active/Accepted (row 2) by how long they've held that status — for example, under 1 year, 1–2 years, 2+ years. Use it to break rows 1–3 down into bands instead of one combined number. Better Impact
12P2Retention Time With Us Before Leaving Groups volunteers who were moved to Archived (row 4) by how long they had been Active/Accepted before leaving. Separates "left early" (possible onboarding issue) from "left after years" (a different, more natural kind of exit). Better Impact
Band boundaries are defaults, not fixed "Under 1 year / 1–2 years / 2+ years" above matches the 1-year cutoff already used in row 1, but it's a proposed starting point, not locked in. These should be built as an adjustable setting, not hard-coded into the measure — so the bands can be tuned later (e.g. to 18 months) without a new requirements document.

05  Why these measures matter

Every row above exists to answer a real question and support a real decision — not just to produce a number. This is that connection made explicit, grouped the same way as Section 02.

Retention

KeyKPI / DimensionBusiness QuestionDecision / Action It Supports
1Active Fosters Over 1 YearHow many of our fosters are long-standing, reliable capacity?Use as a stable floor when planning for upcoming intake — these are the fosters least likely to need backfilling soon.
2Active FostersHow big is our total usable foster pool right now?The baseline for recruitment planning — compare against animal-side demand (Section 06) to see whether a recruitment push is actually needed.
3% Active Fosters Over 1 YearIs our active pool mostly experienced or mostly new?Low and falling → heavy reliance on newer fosters, check onboarding support capacity. High but raw counts (1) flat → growth has stalled even though experience is fine.
4Fosters ArchivedHow many people are we actually losing?The first alarm — a rising raw count is what triggers a closer look via rows 7, 8, and 12, before any program decision is made.
5Not Yet Archived at Start of PeriodHow many people did we have to lose, going into this period?Not an action trigger by itself — it's what makes row 8 meaningful. Without it, row 4's count can't be judged as "a lot" or "a little."
6New Foster CreatedHow many new people are entering the pipeline?Compare against row 4 — if New is falling while Archived holds steady, recruitment needs attention even before attrition looks obviously bad.
7Archived–to–New RatioAre we replacing the people we lose?Persistently over 100% (losing more than we're gaining) → recruitment becomes the priority, not a retention program.
8% Fosters ArchivedRelative to who we had, is attrition getting worse?The real attrition-rate trigger — a rising trend here, not just a rising raw count, is what should prompt a retention intervention, since it's sized against the pool we actually had.
11Time as an Active Foster (dimension)Is our active pool skewing toward new or experienced people?Breaks rows 1–3 into bands — target onboarding support at the newest band specifically, instead of a blanket program aimed at everyone.
12Time With Us Before Leaving (dimension)Are we losing people early, or after years?Early-exit concentration → fix onboarding/matching. Long-tenure concentration → a different problem — burnout or re-engagement for experienced fosters going quiet, not onboarding.

06  Combining with the animal side

The measures above only describe capacity — how many fosters we have. On their own, they can't tell you whether that's enough. For that, we pair them with what ShelterBuddy already tracks on the animal side: Foster Animals (animals currently in foster), Intake Animals (new arrivals that month), Animals In Care (the full population under our care), and % Animal in Foster (Foster Animals ÷ Intake Animals). Reading a Better Impact measure next to one of these answers questions neither side can answer alone.

One limitation to keep in mind There's no confirmed data link yet between a Better Impact volunteer record and a ShelterBuddy record. So for now, this is a side-by-side reading — a person comparing both numbers — not a single report that combines them automatically.

07  Still open

Questions for the Better Impact admin
  • Does "Active/Accepted Date" exist as its own dedicated field, separate from one generic "last status change" date?
  • Does "Archived Date" behave the same way — a dedicated, reliable field?
  • Can a volunteer go from Inactive back to Active/Accepted? If so, is the earlier Active period visible anywhere afterward, or does the date just get overwritten?
  • Can a volunteer be un-Archived (reinstated)? Same question — is anything visible afterward?
  • Does the 5-status lifecycle in Section 01 match BC SPCA's actual configured account?
  • How far back do BC SPCA's actual Better Impact records go?
  • Does a reliable link exist between a Better Impact volunteer record and a ShelterBuddy AnimalID+PersonID placement record? (blocks rows 9–10)
Decisions for the Data Owner
  • Confirmation of the whole measure set — sign-off to proceed.
  • Is 90 days the right window for "recent foster placement" (rows 9–10), or should it be longer or shorter?
