01 · Executive summary

Phase 1 established a trusted historical baseline for foster utilization across BC SPCA. This continuation adds the dimensions required to understand where foster care is used and which animal-type families use it, without changing the approved foster-stay logic.

The solution reconstructs an effective-dated history from three ShelterBuddy histories: source (intake) history, status history, and shelter location (kennel) history. It produces monthly measures of Animals in Care, Distinct Fostered Animals, % Animals in Care Fostered, and Foster Days by effective Centre, Region, and normalized Animal Type Family.

Interpretation boundary. Higher foster utilization is descriptive. It does not establish good or poor performance, capacity sufficiency, unmet demand, better outcomes, or an operational target.
Naming note. % Animals in Care Fostered is a distinct statistic from Phase 1's % Animals in Foster — same intent, different denominator. See Section 08 for the exact distinction before comparing the two reports.

02 · Business problem

The existing organization-wide foster baseline explains historical foster use over time but does not identify the centres, regions, and animal-type families associated with that use. Animal Flow leadership therefore cannot consistently compare foster reliance across operational locations and animal populations using governed, reproducible definitions.

The underlying history is time-dependent. An animal can enter care at one location, move to another centre, change source animal type, experience more than one foster stay, and leave care later. A static current-state join would overwrite historical context and could assign foster activity to the wrong centre or animal type.

Without an effective-dated analysis:

  • centre and animal-type differences cannot be compared reliably;
  • transfers can misattribute foster activity;
  • multiple foster stays can duplicate distinct animals;
  • current animal attributes can rewrite historical months;
  • centre subtotals can be incorrectly added to derive provincial totals; and
  • data-quality gaps can be confused with true zero utilization.

04 · Current state

The delivered Phase 1 baseline provides monthly and annual Fostered Animals and a historical utilization view from January 2006. It also establishes the approved foster-location and foster-end-date rules, data-quality treatment, quarterly refresh, and reproducibility controls.

The current baseline does not yet provide:

  • effective Centre and Region during each month;
  • effective animal type during each month;
  • Animals in Care as the aligned monthly denominator;
  • centre-by-animal-type comparisons;
  • effective-dated transfer and type-change treatment; or
  • dimensional completeness monitoring for Centre and Animal Type.

05 · Desired capability

The future state provides a governed monthly dataset and quarterly aggregate report that:

  • reconstructs valid incoming-to-outgoing care episodes;
  • uses the approved Phase 1 foster-stay logic unchanged;
  • derives the effective Centre from the centre recorded on each status change, keeps the intake Centre from the intake record, and derives Region from a governed Centre-to-Region reference;
  • derives the effective animal type from the intake and status records and normalizes it to a reporting family;
  • counts distinct Animals in Care and Fostered Animals at the aligned monthly dimensional grain;
  • allocates Foster Days to the effective Centre and Animal Type;
  • deduplicates provincial totals independently from dimensional subtotals;
  • reprocesses full history quarterly and records any historical restatement; and
  • exposes unresolved mappings and source conflicts instead of guessing.

07 · Analytical approach

7.1 Monthly analytical grain

Reporting Month Animal ID Care Episode ID Effective Centre Effective Region Source Animal Type ID and Label Normalized Animal Type Family Is Animal in Care Is Fostered Animal Foster Days

Within a dimensional group, the distinct-count key is:

Reporting Month + Animal ID + Effective Centre + Normalized Animal Type Family

For organization-wide monthly totals, the distinct-count key is:

Reporting Month + Animal ID
Non-additivity control. Centre, Region, and Animal Type subtotals must not be added to derive the organization-wide distinct total. An animal can validly appear in more than one subgroup during a month.

7.2 Care episodes

  • Each intake creates a source record in ShelterBuddy. Every later status change is recorded against that source record, so one intake links to many status changes.
  • Only an incoming source record opens a care episode (42 source types, such as Stray, Owner Surrender, Humane Officer and Transfer In). This is the same rule Phase 1 uses to count intakes and to define the foster population.
  • Service records are ignored. Non-incoming source types (Lost, Found, Clinic, Boarding, Microchip, License, Dispatch Information, Data Import and others) do not open, close or change a care episode.
  • The episode closes at the first Outgoing status recorded against it (Adopted, Transfer Out, Returned To Owner, Euthanized and others), as defined by ShelterBuddy's status terms.
  • Internal transfers do not close an episode. Internal Transfer Out and Internal Transfer In are In Care statuses: the animal stays in BC SPCA care and changes centre.
  • A later incoming source record opens a new care episode.
  • An episode with no Outgoing status continues through the reporting cutoff.
  • A foster stay is attached to the incoming episode in effect when it starts. In the rare case where the intake was keyed after the foster move (0.05% of foster stays), it is attached to the next incoming intake, so province-wide Fostered Animals still equal Phase 1 exactly.

7.3 Foster stays

  • Use the approved Phase 1 foster definition without modification.
  • Each Shelter Location History row begins at its change timestamp and ends at the next location change.
  • A location containing "foster" qualifies, excluding Foster to Adopt.
  • A foster end timestamp is exclusive. The day the animal leaves foster is not a foster day.
  • A qualifying same-day foster stay still counts.
  • Existing Phase 1 resolver rules remain authoritative for latest foster locations without a recorded end.

7.4 Effective centre and animal type

Two centres are recorded, because they answer different questions:

CentreWhere it comes fromChanges during the episode?Answers
Intake CentreThe centre on the intake (source) recordNoWhich centre took the animal in?
Effective CentreThe centre on the most recent status change. Before the first status change, the intake centreYes, at each transferWhich centre held the animal that day or month?
  • Transfers are common: about 12% of intakes are held by more than one centre during the episode. Monthly results therefore follow the Effective Centre (open decision E1).
  • ShelterBuddy labels the centre as Region on status records and Physical Location on intake records. In this BRD it is the Centre. Region is a group of centres taken from a governed reference list; it does not exist in ShelterBuddy.
  • Shelter Location (kennel) history records rooms (Foster, Dog Kennels…), not centres. A foster stay therefore takes its centre from the status changes around it.
  • The effective animal type comes from the most recent status change, or from the intake record before the first status change.
  • Kitten and Cat normalize to Cat; Puppy and Dog normalize to Dog. Most type changes in ShelterBuddy are Kitten to Cat or Puppy to Dog, so they do not move the animal to a different family.
  • All other types map to the reporting families in Appendix B.
  • Unresolved Centre is Unmapped. Unresolved animal type is Unknown.
  • Historical months are not overwritten with the latest Centre or Animal Type.

The record-level rules (link fields, tie-breakers, profiling results) are in the technical definition: Care Episode, Centre and Animal Type Definition v0.1, included as Appendix F.

7.5 Monthly overlap

Use Vancouver local time and interval-overlap logic. Count an animal as in care when the care episode overlaps at least one calendar day of the month. Count a Fostered Animal when a qualifying foster stay overlaps at least one day of the month.

08 · Business definitions and KPI contracts

Animals in Care

Definition: Distinct animals whose valid incoming-to-outgoing care episode overlaps at least one day of the reporting month.

DISTINCTCOUNT(Animal ID)

Grain: Month + Centre + Animal Type Family.

Owner: Animal Flow Manager.

Unknown treatment: Preserve Unmapped and Unknown groups.

Fostered Animals

Definition: Distinct Animals in Care that overlap a qualifying foster stay for at least one day in the reporting month.

DISTINCTCOUNT(Animal ID where Is Fostered Animal = true)

Grain: Month + Centre + Animal Type Family.

Owner: Animal Flow Manager.

Reconciliation: Provincial monthly totals must equal Phase 1.

% Animals in Care Fostered

Definition: The percentage of distinct Animals in Care that experience qualifying foster care in the aligned month and dimensional group.

Fostered Animals ÷ Animals in Care × 100

Type: Descriptive utilization measure.

Interpretation: Not a performance, capacity, demand, outcome, or target measure.

Owner: Animal Flow Manager.

Foster Days

Definition: Calendar days of qualifying foster care allocated to the Centre and Animal Type effective during each dated segment.

SUM(Qualifying foster-day overlap)

Grain: Effective-dated segment rolled to month.

Owner: Animal Flow Manager.

Control: Segment days must not overlap or duplicate.

Distinct from Phase 1's "% Animals in Foster."

Phase 1 defines % Animals in Foster as Fostered Animals ÷ Intake Animals — the distinct animals with a new intake dated in the period. This measure, % Animals in Care Fostered, divides by Animals in Care instead — the full population active at any point in the month, including animals that arrived in an earlier period. Animals in Care is always equal to or larger than Intake Animals for the same month, so the two percentages will not match even though the numerator (Fostered Animals) reconciles exactly between the two reports. Treat them as two different statistics describing two different denominators, not as the same measure recomputed at finer grain.

10.2 Event ordering

  • Use Vancouver local timestamps.
  • Retain source record identifiers and sequence fields for audit.
  • Apply all cross-history changes sharing a timestamp before generating the next effective segment.
  • When records within the same history share a timestamp, use the source sequence or unique record ID as deterministic tie-breaker.
  • An Outgoing event closes the care episode. Later location, kennel, or type records cannot create in-care or foster activity unless a new valid Incoming event opens another episode.
  • When no reliable order exists and alternatives materially change results, classify the interval as Data Conflict.

12 · Assumptions, constraints, and accepted limitations

12.1 Assumptions

  • ShelterBuddy histories remain the authoritative operational evidence.
  • The approved Phase 1 foster-stay and end-date resolver rules remain valid.
  • The centre recorded on ShelterBuddy status changes reflects the centre holding the animal at that time. Region is derived from Centre with governed reference data.
  • Quarterly source extracts contain stable unique record identifiers or deterministic sequence fields.

12.2 Constraints

  • Distinct counts across Centre and Animal Type are non-additive.
  • Historical source corrections can restate previously published months.
  • Current-quarter delivery depends on successful source refresh, full-history processing, and validation.
  • Low-volume filters can make otherwise reportable groups ineligible for comparative ranking.

12.3 Accepted limitations

  • Up to 1% Unmapped or Unknown dimensional records may be carried with explicit business-owner acceptance.
  • Data Conflict intervals remain visible and are not resolved by unsupported assumptions.
  • Whether true Centre × Animal Type zero-utilization combinations exist remains a source-profiling fact.
  • Age-stage distinctions are intentionally normalized and are not analyzed in this phase.

14 · Data quality and validation

14.1 Completeness threshold

At least 99% of monthly Animals in Care records and Fostered Animal records must have both an effective Centre and a normalized Animal Type Family.

  • Unmapped Centre and Unknown Animal Type remain in province-wide totals when underlying care and foster state are reliable.
  • Unresolved dimensions are excluded from named-category rankings.
  • Publication below 99% requires explicit business-owner acceptance.

14.2 Required validation

  1. Reconcile province-wide monthly Fostered Animals exactly to the approved Phase 1 output.
  2. Validate incoming-to-outgoing care episodes and open-episode cutoff behavior.
  3. Validate foster-state results against approved worked examples and resolver rules.
  4. Validate Centre transfer, mid-month Centre change, and multiple-centre attribution.
  5. Validate Kitten/Cat and Puppy/Dog normalization.
  6. Validate multiple foster stays with no duplicate monthly distinct animal.
  7. Validate same-day foster stays and exclusive foster end timestamps.
  8. Validate Unmapped, Unknown, and Data Conflict treatment.
  9. Validate true zero-utilization groups, if present, from Animals in Care rather than a foster-only dataset.
  10. Repeat a quarterly run against unchanged source data and reproduce the same result.

14.3 Restatement controls

  • Reprocess complete history from January 2006 each quarter.
  • Compare current results with the previously published snapshot by month and measure.
  • Log affected months, measures, source records, and reason for every historical change.
  • Disclose material restatements in the next quarterly report.
  • Retain prior published snapshots for audit.
  • Do not preserve a known incorrect result merely to match a prior PDF.

Appendix F · Technical definition: care episodes, centre and animal type

Technical reference.

Record-level rules for implementation and validation, from Care Episode, Centre and Animal Type Definition v0.1. Business rules are in Sections 07 and 08; open decisions are in Appendix E. Where this appendix and the business sections differ, the business sections govern and this appendix is corrected.

F.1 · Why this definition exists

The next analysis compares foster use by centre and animal type:

% Animals in Care Fostered = Fostered Animals ÷ Animals in Care, by month, centre and animal type family.

This is not Phase 1's % Animals in Foster, which divides by Intake Animals (see Section 08).

For that we need three facts for every animal on every day it was with us:

  1. Was it in our care? That gives the denominator: Animals in Care.
  2. Which centre held it?
  3. What type of animal was it?

The foster stay itself (start, end, Foster to Adopt excluded, end-date resolver, 365-day cap) is unchanged from Phase 1. See Foster_Episode_Definition_v0_1.md. This document only adds the care episode, the centre and the animal type around it.

F.2 · Where the data comes from, and how it links

ShelterBuddy's process (SOP):

  • Intake. Staff create a source record. It holds the animal, the source (Stray, Owner Surrender…), the animal type and the centre that took the animal in.
  • Every later change. Each change is a status record linked to that source record. A status record holds the status, the centre holding the animal at that time and the animal type.
  • Room moves. These are recorded separately in Shelter Location (kennel) history.
ShelterBuddy tableOne row isGives usKey fields

Status HistoryOne status changeEpisode end (Outgoing), centre over time, type over timeIntakeExtendedDetailsId, StatusDateUtc, StatusId → IsOutgoing, RegionName, AnimalTypeName
Shelter Location History (kennel)One room moveFoster stays (Phase 1 logic)AnimalId, AddedDateTimeUtc, ShelterLocationName

The link: status IntakeExtendedDetailsId = source history Id. It is one-to-many: one intake, many status changes.

Watch out: status history also has IntakeSourceId. That is the source type (e.g. 14 = Stray), the same value as the source record's SourceId. It is not the record key.

Naming: ShelterBuddy labels the centre as "Region" (RegionName) on status history and "Physical Location" on source history. Both hold the same list of centres (Vancouver, Kelowna, qathet…). In this analysis that is the Centre. A Region (a group of centres) does not exist in ShelterBuddy and comes from a reference list (section 10).

Shelter Location (kennel) history has no centre. ShelterLocationName is a room name (Foster, Dog Kennels, Cat Intake Room…), so a foster stay gets its centre from status history.

F.3 · Step 1: Care episodes (Animals in Care)

A care episode starts at an incoming source record and ends at the first Outgoing status linked to it.

  • Incoming only. Only source types with IsIncoming = TRUE open an episode. There are 42 types, e.g. Stray, Owner Surrender, Humane Officer, Transfer In, Foster Offspring, Emergency Boarding. This is the same filter Phase 1 uses for intakes and for the foster population.
  • Service records are ignored. The 16 non-incoming types (Lost, Found, Clinic, Boarding, Microchip, License, Dispatch Information, Identification & Services, Data Import, API Entry…) do not open, close or change an episode.
  • End: the first status with IsOutgoing = TRUE (Adopted, Transfer Out, Returned To Owner, Euthanized…) among the status rows linked to the episode. With no Outgoing status, the episode is open and runs to the report date.
  • Internal transfers do not end an episode. Internal Transfer Out (Id 153) and Internal Transfer In (Id 154) are In Care. They are not returned by the ShelterBuddy status list, so nb_sb_api_brz_status adds them as manual rows. They are frequent (about 96,000 each), and they are how a centre change shows up.
  • New intake, new episode. An animal that comes back later gets a new incoming source record and so a new episode.

F.4 · Step 2: Centre

Two centres are kept, because they answer different questions:

CentreRuleChanges during the episode?Answers
Intake centreSource record PhysicalLocationNameNoWhich centre took the animal in
Effective centreRegionName on the latest status row at or before the moment. Before the first status, the intake centreYes, at each transferWhich centre held the animal that day or month
  • Transfers are common. Statuses span more than one centre in 78,539 of 667,101 source records (11.8%). Status RegionName differs from source PhysicalLocationName on 19% of status rows. Typical moves are North Cariboo, South Peace and Prince Rupert to Quesnel, and Williams Lake and Chilliwack to Vancouver.
  • Ties: when status rows share a timestamp, the higher status Id wins.
  • Not every "centre" is a shelter: Animal Protection Services, Prince George Spay/Neuter Clinic, Kamloops Spay/Neuter Clinic, Clinic (Inactive), Prov. Office, Penticton Veterinary Hospital, Wild ARC. Treatment is a Data Owner decision (section 10).
  • Missing: a blank centre is reported as Unmapped. It stays in province-wide totals.

F.5 · Step 3: Animal type and family

  • Effective type = AnimalTypeName on the latest status row at or before the moment. Before the first status, the source record's type.
  • Family = the reporting group below. Kitten and Puppy are separate ShelterBuddy types, but they are the same family as Cat and Dog.

How often the type changes: 18,732 of 592,614 animals have more than one type. About 18,300 of those are Kitten → Cat or Puppy → Dog, which does not change the family. True family changes (e.g. Cat ↔ Dog, 125 animals) are under 0.1% and look like data corrections.

F.6 · Step 4: Attaching foster stays

Foster stays come from Shelter Location history, exactly as in Phase 1:

  • the location contains "foster" and is not Foster to Adopt;
  • end = the next location change, or the Rule 1 / Rule 2 resolver;
  • unresolved stays are excluded;
  • the report caps a stay at 365 days.

Each foster stay is then given:

AttributeRule
Care episodeThe incoming episode in effect at the foster start, i.e. the latest incoming source record at or before the start
CentreEffective centre on each day of the stay. A transfer during foster moves the remaining foster days to the new centre
Animal type familyEffective family on each day of the stay

Foster stays with no earlier incoming intake. These are 66 of 130,726 foster rows (0.05%). The usual cause is that the intake was keyed after the foster move. They attach to the animal's next incoming intake, so province-wide Fostered Animals still equal Phase 1 exactly.

The Phase 1 population rule stays as it is: the animal has at least one incoming intake (IsIncoming = 1 in nb_sb_silver_kennel_history_foster_adjust, Block 6).

F.7 · Step 5: Monthly counting

All dates are in Vancouver time (America/Vancouver). An interval counts in a month if it overlaps at least one calendar day of that month. End dates are exclusive, so the day an animal leaves is not counted. A same-day stay still counts.

MeasureRuleGrain
Animals in CareDistinct AnimalId with a care episode overlapping the monthMonth + Centre + Family
Fostered AnimalsDistinct AnimalId with a foster stay overlapping the monthMonth + Centre + Family
% Animals in Care FosteredFostered Animals ÷ Animals in Care × 100Same group, same month
Foster DaysDays of foster in the month, allocated to the centre and family in effect on each daySummed

Not additive. An animal that changes centre (or family) during a month counts once in each centre. Adding centres together therefore over-counts. The province-wide total is a separate distinct count (Month + AnimalId), never the sum of centres. Foster Days are additive, because each day goes to exactly one centre.
