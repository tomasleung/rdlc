02Our Philosophy

Traditional BI asks

  • What charts?
  • What KPIs?
  • What filters?

Decision-Driven BI asks

  • What decision needs to be made?
  • Who owns the decision?
  • What outcome are we trying to achieve?

Technology comes later.

04The 7 Analytical Layers

Layer 1What happened?
ExamplesIntake count, Foster count, Capacity count
Analysis TypeDescriptive
Layer 2Did something unusual happen?
ExamplesCapacity spike, Foster decline, Intake surge
Analysis TypeDescriptive + Statistical Monitoring
Possible MethodsNormal ApproximationControl LimitsAnomaly Detection
Layer 3What is related?
ExamplesFoster rate vs. intake, Capacity vs. transfers
Analysis TypeDiagnostic
Possible MethodsCorrelationSegmentationDrill-through
Layer 4What is driving it?
ExamplesWhy is capacity increasing? Why do animals remain longer?
Analysis TypePredictive
Possible MethodsRegressionFeature ImportanceTrend Analysis
Layer 5What should we focus on?
ExamplesCentre requiring intervention, Program requiring attention
Analysis TypeDecision Support
OutputPrioritized opportunities, Recommended focus areas
Layer 6What should we do?
ExamplesIncrease transfers, Recruit foster homes, Escalate Animal Flow review
Analysis TypePrescriptive
OutputRecommended actions
Layer 7Did it work?
ExamplesCapacity reduced, Foster participation increased, Placement time decreased
Analysis TypeOutcome Measurement

10Framework Statement

Official Positioning
Decision-Driven BI & AI is a framework for helping organizations move from business problems to measurable outcomes by combining analytics, data products, governance, and AI-assisted delivery.

Rather than focusing on reports, dashboards, or technology, the framework focuses on decisions, actions, and outcomes.
Reports are outputs.
Data Products are capabilities.
Outcomes are the goal.
