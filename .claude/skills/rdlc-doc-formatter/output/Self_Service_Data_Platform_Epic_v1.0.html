01Epic Summary

Problem
Departments depend on the Data Team to onboard and analyze every new data source, creating a delivery bottleneck. Small departmental requests compete with enterprise initiatives, business users can't experiment independently, and the Data Team spends time on low-value integration work instead of enterprise data products.
Objective
Provide a governed Microsoft Fabric platform where the Data Team owns provisioning, governance, security, and Bronze ingestion patterns, while departments independently develop Silver, Gold, semantic models, and reporting for their own data.
Scope
See the Data Team / Department split below — this epic delivers the platform and guardrails, not any one department's analytics solution.
Ask
Approval to provision the first self-service workspace as a pilot with Marketing (Mailchimp), before offering it more broadly to other departments.
Timeline
No hard deadline at this stage. The Mailchimp pilot begins once approved; a broader rollout timeline will be set after pilot results are in.
Governance Guardrail
Self-service operates inside existing Fabric governance, not around it: every workspace is provisioned under the same security model, Entra group structure, and capacity monitoring the Data Team already maintains for Managed Data Products. The Data Team retains Bronze ingestion ownership, so there is always one governed landing point per source — departments build Silver/Gold on top of it, not a parallel ungoverned pipeline.
Business Outcome
Faster access to data, reduced delivery bottlenecks, improved scalability, and a pathway for identifying future enterprise-wide Managed Data Products.

Scope: who owns what

Data Team owns

  • Fabric capacity and workspace governance
  • Security model and Entra group configuration
  • Bronze ingestion patterns per source
  • Shared platform standards

Department owns

  • Silver and Gold transformations
  • Business rules and semantic models
  • Reports and downstream analytics
  • Ongoing maintenance of their own solution

02Supporting ROI Rationale

Background for the epic, not part of the Asana description itself — condensed from your ROI Drivers section.

1

Faster time to insight

Departments get a workspace and Bronze data directly, instead of waiting in a backlog for Data Team development.

2

Reduced Data Team bottlenecks

The Data Team stops building one-off departmental integrations (Mailchimp, SurveyMonkey, volunteer systems) and focuses on Fabric governance and enterprise data products.

3

Increased data adoption

Lower effort to start means more teams become data consumers and more business-led innovation occurs.

4

Lower cost of analytics delivery

One platform — Fabric capacity, governance, security, workspace process — supports many departments instead of a custom solution per department.

5

Pipeline for future data products

The strongest argument in the case. Self-service is the incubation layer: a department's self-service solution that later needs cross-system integration or multi-team consumption graduates into a Managed Data Product — see the Mailchimp example below.

Worked example: Marketing / Mailchimp

Phase 1 — Self-Service
  • Marketing wants Mailchimp data
  • Data Team lands Mailchimp into Bronze
  • Marketing builds its own Silver transformations
→
Phase 2 — Candidate for Managed Data Product
  • Marketing wants Mailchimp + Raiser's Edge integration
  • Multiple teams begin consuming the data
  • Graduates from Self-Service into a Managed Data Product
