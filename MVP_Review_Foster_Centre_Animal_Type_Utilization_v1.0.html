The three things to walk through

This is the actual MVP conversation — not whether the charts look right, but whether we own the right problem and whether the data genuinely answers it.

1. What data problem do we own?

In scope: Animals in Care, Fostered Animals, % Animals in Care Fostered, and Foster Days — by effective Centre, Region, and three Animal Type families (Cat, Dog, Small Animal) — month by month, January 2006 to the latest complete quarter.

Out of scope or deferred: Municipality-level geography (no source data yet), Age Group, any Animal Type beyond the three confirmed families, and — pending your answer above — seasonality and directional transfer.

2. How does the data answer those questions?

Business question → what answers it → in plain language
Business questionRegister entryIn plain language
In-care & fostered volumes, by month/Centre/Region/TypeCATU-BASE-01The full 2006–latest history, not just this year — so a reader sees the real baseline before judging anything recent.
Which centres/regions use foster mostCATU-CENTRE-01 / CATU-REGION-01Ranked, with a minimum-volume floor so a centre with 3 animals can't out-rank one with 300.
Which animal types use foster mostCATU-TYPE-01Same ranking logic, by Cat/Dog/Small Animal instead of Centre.
Is a Centre difference real, or just its animal mixCATU-CENTRETYPE-01Splits a Centre's gap into "it has more cats" (mix) versus "it fosters cats at a genuinely higher rate" (rate) — the two have very different implications.
Where are changes occurring over timeCATU-TREND-01 / CATU-CHANGE-01Year-over-year, with the change broken down by which Centre/Type groups actually drove it.
How often animals relate to multiple centresCATU-TRANSFER-01 / CATU-TRANSFER-DIR-01First, a disclosure that Centre totals won't add up perfectly. Second (new, open), which centres send to which.
What data-quality gaps affect interpretationCATU-DQ-01A plain completeness rate against the 99% floor the BRD set, not hidden inside the other numbers.

3. How does this support data-driven analysis going forward?

The same handful of methods now answers several different cross-cuts, instead of a new one-off chart being invented for each: mix-adjustment separates "fosters more animals" from "just has more cats," decomposition attributes a change to the groups that actually drove it, and a minimum-volume floor is applied everywhere a ranking appears — including the new directional-transfer view, which reused the same effective-dated history already built for Effective Centre rather than needing new source data. That's a reusable framework the next Driver or Phase can draw on, not a one-time report.
