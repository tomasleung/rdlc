Where was each animal, and what type was it?

20-year audit: stays from January 2005 to September 2026Prepared for the ShelterBuddy Data OwnerDraft v0.2 · 30 October 2026

Report 1 set when each animal came into our care and when it left. This report checks where it was and what type it was during each stay, so Animals in Care can be counted by centre and animal type. 98.2% of stays are recorded as expected, and the centre is known for 99.95% of animal-months. Foster stays, which have no centre or type of their own, take them from the care stay they fall in: 99.3% of foster records fit a care stay directly. The main decisions are about mapping: who owns protective custody animals, how to report non-shelter centres, which animal types to report, and the few foster records outside a care stay.

What we want to confirm

For every day an animal was in our care, we want to know which centre held it and what type of animal it was. Report 1 fixed the stays (intake to outcome); this report reads the centre and type from the records inside each stay.

What we expect, based on the SOP

The intake records the centre that took the animal in and its type. Every later status records the centre holding the animal and its type at that moment, so a transfer shows as a status at the new centre. Both use the same lists: the intake's Physical Location and the status's Region are the same centres.

ShelterBuddy calls the centre Region on status records. In this report it is the Centre.

How we tested the data

  1. The full history, not a sample: every stay from report 1 (524,373) and every status inside it.
  2. Read in order: the intake, then each status in the order ShelterBuddy shows it, up to the outcome. A new segment starts when the centre or the type family changes (634,807 segments).
  3. Counted twice: by stay (one group each, in the summary) and by animal-month (completeness, families, two centres in one month).
  4. Checked by hand in ShelterBuddy: each group has an example animal below.
  5. One pass, repeatable: the scripts are kept with the project (analysis/phase2) and will run in Microsoft Fabric before sign-off.

Why it matters: an animal can be at two centres in one month

When an animal is transferred, it is in care at two centres in the same month. It counts once at each centre, but only once in the province-wide total, so centre totals add up to more than the province total. Transfers are increasing: 4.4% of animal-months in 2006, 13.7% in 2026.

The six rules, with examples

Why six rules

Centre and type come from the records inside each stay. Each rule answers one question: where the stay starts (Rule 1), how the centre changes (Rule 2), how the type changes (Rule 3), what happens to blanks (Rule 4), which records count (Rule 5), and how foster stays get a centre and type (Rule 6).

Rule 1

The intake gives the centre and type at the start

Where was the animal, and what type was it, when it came in?

Rule

The stay starts at the centre and animal type on the intake (source PhysicalLocationId, AnimalTypeId). If the intake has no centre, the centre on the first status in the stay is used.

Rule 2

Each status inside the stay gives the centre from that moment

Where was the animal on each day of the stay?

Rule

Every status inside the stay carries the centre holding the animal (status RegionId, the same list as the intake centre). From each status on, the animal is at that status's centre, until another status shows a different one.

Rule

Rule 4

A blank value carries forward; unknown values stay in the totals

What if a status has no centre or type?

Rule

A status with a blank centre or type keeps the last known value. If nothing in the stay has a centre, it is reported as Unmapped (type: Unknown) and still counted in province-wide totals.

Rule 5

Only records inside the stay count

Can a status outside the stay change the centre?

Rule

Only statuses inside the stay (from the intake up to and including its outcome) set the centre and type. Statuses after the outcome are ignored, as in report 1.

CaseStaysTreatment
Statuses inside a stay2,123,658Used
Statuses after the outcomesee report 1, Rule 5Ignored

Why: A status keyed against an old intake cannot move the animal: the stay it belongs to has already ended.

Rule 6

A foster stay takes the centre and type of the care stay it falls in

Phase 1 foster stays come from location (kennel) history, which has no centre and no type. Does every foster stay fit a care stay?

Rule

The foster stay is found exactly as in Phase 1. It takes the centre and type of the care stay at that time; a transfer while in foster moves the remaining foster time to the new centre. A foster stay outside every care stay takes the animal's previous stay at its end, else its next stay at its start, and also counts as time in care.

Mapping decisions

The data is complete and consistent. What remains are reporting choices for the data owner.

Decision A

Animal type families

The MVP reports three families: Cat, Dog and Small Animal. They cover 89.5% of animal-months. The rest, Rabbit (3.6%) and Other (6.8%), would count as Unknown and fail the BRD's 99% floor unless they are given a family.

Proposal: report five families, Cat, Dog, Small Animal, Rabbit and Other (birds, farm animals, wildlife, reptiles…), so every animal is counted; Other is shown but not ranked.

Decision B

Centres that are not shelters

Animal Protection Services holds animals in protective custody and emergency boarding, often alongside a shelter (see Rule 2, animal 623743). Proposal: report these as their own rows, outside the centre rankings, and keep them in province totals; the data owner says whether protective-custody animals should instead be counted at the shelter holding them.

Decision C

Centres that opened or closed

34 centres took intakes in 2025. These stopped taking intakes:

  • Delta (last intake 2006)
  • Coquitlam (last intake 2008)
  • Boundary (last intake 2009)
  • 100 Mile House (last intake 2022)
  • Haida Gwaii (last intake 2023)
  • Nelson (last intake 2024)

Proposal: show each centre's "data available from / until" years on centre trends; the full list is in the data tables below. Kitimat appears only on status records (a transfer destination).
