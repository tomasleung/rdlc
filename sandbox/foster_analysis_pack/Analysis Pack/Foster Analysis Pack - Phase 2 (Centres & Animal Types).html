Why

Why Animals in Care comes first

Phase 1 counts Fostered Animals from the location (kennel) history. That history gives the foster dates, but no centre and no animal type. Phase 1's % Animals in Foster also divides by Intake Animals: only the animals that came in during the period, not all the animals in our care.

To compare foster use across centres and animal types, we need the right denominator, Animals in Care, and a centre and type for every day. Only two ShelterBuddy histories record these: the Source History (intakes) and the Status History. So everything is built from them, in this order:

1 · RecordsIntake and outcomeSource History gives the day the animal came in; Status History the day it left
2 · StaysAnimals in CareEach stay runs from an intake to its outcome
3 · Inside each stayCentre and animal typeThe intake, then each status in the stay, set the centre and type day by day
4 · FosterFostered AnimalsEach Phase 1 foster stay takes the centre and type of the care day it falls on
Why the stay logic has to be right first
  • A stay left open counts an animal for ever. Without the rules, about 1,900 animals that left years ago would be counted in care every month.
  • A wrong stay gives a wrong centre. The centre and type are read only from records inside the stay; a record after the outcome must not move the animal.
  • Foster must sit inside care. Every fostered animal must also be an animal in care, or the share fostered could exceed 100%.
  • Only animals that can be fostered should count. Dead-on-arrival, end-of-life and wildlife animals are never fostered. Left in, they lower the rate of the centres that take them in. The scope filters (steps 5 and 6) apply to Animals in Care and Fostered Animals alike.

The measure: % Animals in Care Fostered = Fostered Animals ÷ Animals in Care, for the same month or year, centre and animal type.

Part 3

What we found so far First draft

Before the rules above are approved, we ran a first analysis with the simplest logic: each fostered animal takes the centre and animal type of its latest intake (Source History only), and the Phase 1 measure, % Animals in Foster = Fostered Animals ÷ Intake Animals. It is not yet % Animals in Care Fostered, and no scope filter is applied.

  1. Intake fell far more than fostering. Since 2006 intake animals are down 65% and fostered animals 25%, so % Animals in Foster rose from 14.4% to 31.1% in 2025 (4,314 of 13,863). Since 2022, though, fostering has fallen faster than intake (-22% against -8%).
  2. Cats drive foster. Cats are 62.8% of intake but 72.3% of fostered animals; Cat 35.8%, Dog 24.5% in 2025. A centre's mix matters: compare centres within an animal type (Cat with Cat), not on their totals.
  3. Across the 34 active centres the median rate is 31.5%; most range from 12.4% to 75.6% (Vernon, North Cariboo and Tri-Cities (1, 12 and 24 intake animals) have too few intakes for a rate to mean much). Ten centres hold 52.7% of fostered animals. 8 centres combine high demand with a high rate; Maple Ridge, South Peace, Williams Lake and Quesnel have high demand but a low rate.
  4. Scope matters for some centres. 31% of Richmond's 2025 intake was dead on arrival, which is never fostered: its rate is 13.5% with DOA and 19.5% without (D3).
  5. One known artefact. North Cariboo shows 125%: its intake fell to 12 animals in 2025, but 15 animals fostered in 2025 are credited to it, 14 of them taken in during 2024. The Animals in Care measure removes this, since those animals were in care in 2025.
Why the first draft is good enough to read
  • 96.4% of intakes are clean as entered, and 99.3% of foster records fit a care stay (Parts 1 and 2).
  • The animal type is the same in the first draft and the Phase 2 care-stay view for 100.0% of animals fostered in 2025: type and province figures will hardly move.
  • The centre is the same for 84.8%. The other 15.0% were transferred before or during foster, so centre figures will shift: Vancouver, Surrey, Tri-Cities, qathet gain; Kamloops, Quesnel, Williams Lake, South Peace lose.
