How reliable is ShelterBuddy's intake-to-outcome history?

20-year audit: intakes from January 2005 to September 2026Prepared for the ShelterBuddy Data OwnerDraft v0.3 · 29 October 2026

To count Animals in Care, we need to know when each animal came into our care and when it left. ShelterBuddy records this as one intake with one outcome. 96.4% of intakes are clean as entered, and 99.0% have exactly one outcome. The rest are mostly history from before 2013, but a few thousand old intakes were never closed: left uncorrected, they would add about 1,900 animals who left years ago to every month's count.

What we want to confirm

For every animal, we use ShelterBuddy's source (intake) history and status history to confirm when the animal came into our care and when it left. This care timeline is the basis for counting Animals in Care.

What we expect, based on the SOP

When an animal comes to us, staff create one intake record. While it is in our care its status changes (Stray Hold, Available for Adoption, In Foster…), each change linked to that intake. Eventually the status changes to an outcome (an Outgoing status: Adopted, Redeemed, Transfer Out…) and the animal has left. So each intake should have exactly one outcome.

Returns. Since 2013 an animal that comes back gets a new intake (a Returns intake). Before 2013 staff often changed the status on the old intake from the outcome back to an in-care status instead, so the return has no intake of its own. When such a return also ends with its own outcome, it is counted as a stay of its own.

What we tested. We checked this expectation against 20 years of records and grouped every difference into five questions. Centre and animal type are on every intake and status record, so once the stays are set they follow directly; they are covered in the next report.

How we tested the data

  1. The full history, not a sample: every incoming intake from January 2005 to September 2026 (524,493) and every linked status record (2,123,658).
  2. Counted by outcome: every intake is placed in exactly one group by how many outcomes it has: none, one, or two or more.
  3. Checked by hand in ShelterBuddy: for each group we opened examples (Source History, Status History, Edit Animal Details). This changed the rules twice: animal 287927 (crossed back-entry dates, so early statuses are flagged, not corrected) and animal 544551 (statuses stamped 00:00 must be ordered by when they were keyed).
  4. Calendar dates: before 2013 status times were stored as dates only, so only a status on an earlier date than its intake counts as an exception.
  5. One pass, repeatable: all rules are applied together and the groups add up exactly to 524,493. The same logic will run in Microsoft Fabric before sign-off.

Why it matters: Animals in Care

An intake with no outcome keeps the animal "in care" every month from then on, so these ghost animals are carried forward into every later year. They are not records from the year shown: most are intakes from 2005 to 2012. The last column shows how few new unclosed intakes each year adds.

  • Ghost animals build up from 428 a month in 2006 to about 1,800 by 2019. Each year until 2020 added 30 to 60 more; since 2021 almost none.
  • In 2026 they would add 1,928 animals to a real population of 2,226, nearly doubling the count.

The five rules, with examples

Why five rules

The normal case is one intake, one outcome. Each rule answers one question about where a record differs: no status at all (Rule 1), a status dated too early (Rule 2), two or more outcomes (Rule 3), no outcome (Rule 4), or more records after the one outcome (Rule 5).

Rule 1

An intake with no status records is not used

Does every intake have status records linked to it?

Rule

An intake with no status records is discarded: with no status there is no outcome and no stay to build.

Rule 2

The intake date is when the animal came in

Is every status dated on or after its intake?

Rule

The animal came in on its intake date. A status dated earlier is treated as on the intake date; if the outcome is dated earlier, the animal came in and left the same day. A status more than 30 days before its intake is flagged.

Rule 3

Two or more outcomes on one intake: in a row, the first is used; with a return between, each is its own stay

Does each intake have exactly one outcome?

Rule

When one intake has two outcomes in a row (no In Care status between them), the stay ends at the first outcome. Which outcome is right is for the data owner to confirm; the full list is provided.

When an outcome is followed by an In Care status and then its own outcome (outcome → In Care → outcome), the animal came back and the return was recorded on the same intake. Each pair is its own stay: stay 2 runs from that In Care status to its outcome. This applies only if the return starts before the animal's next intake; otherwise the next intake already covers it.

Rule 4

No outcome: still in care, or presumed closed after 365 days

What if an intake never gets an outcome?

Rule

If the intake is 365 days old or less, has no later intake, and its latest status is In Care, the animal is still in care. Otherwise the intake is presumed closed: at the animal's next intake if it has one, otherwise at its last status (never at "intake + 365 days", which would invent care time). If the intake is more than 365 days old and its last status still says In Care, it is flagged.

The 365 days is the intake's age at the report date, not the length of the stay. Most closed intakes are short: for example, animal 5189's intake 4932 is closed 13 minutes later by its next intake (the intake was keyed again).

Rule 5

Records after the outcome are ignored

What if more status records follow the one outcome?

Rule

When an intake has one outcome and more status records follow it, the stay ends at the outcome and the later records are ignored. They are either a note (an Other status: neither In Care nor Outgoing) or an In Care status that never gets its own outcome. (An In Care status that does get its own outcome is a return: a new stay, see Rule 3.)

Duplicate intakes: small, but worth fixing

Since 2022 there are 43 cases of the same animal with two intakes of the same source type on the same day, a median of 51 minutes apart. In 42 of them both intakes are closed with their own outcome: the same event appears to have been entered twice. Animals in Care is not affected (the same animal on the same day counts once), but any count of intake or outcome records would count it twice.

Going forward: a quarterly check

The rules run with every quarterly refresh. Each quarter the data owner receives only the new exceptions: intakes with two outcomes, intakes with no outcome after 365 days, statuses more than 30 days before their intake, and duplicate intakes. At today's rates that is a handful of records a quarter.
