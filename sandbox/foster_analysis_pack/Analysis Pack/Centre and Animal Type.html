Foster Pathway Analytics · Centre and animal type definition

How we assign centre and animal type

Version 0.1Draft for Data Owner reviewOctober 2026

Animals in Care and Fostered Animals tell us when an animal was with us. To compare foster use across centres and animal types, we also need where it was and what type it was on each of those days. Phase 1 could not do this: the location (kennel) history that gives foster stays has no centre and no animal type. The Source History and Status History do, and now that every stay is built from them, the same records give the centre and type for every day, including every foster day.

The rule in one sentence

On every day of a stay, the animal is at the centre and of the type shown by the latest record in that stay: first the intake, then each status. A foster day takes the centre and type of the care day it falls on.
  • Centre: the centre holding the animal (ShelterBuddy calls it Physical Location on the intake and Region on a status; the same list of centres).
  • Animal type: ShelterBuddy's animal type (Cat, Kitten, Dog…), reported by family, so a kitten that grows into a cat stays in one family.
  • Changes: a transfer, or any status at a new centre, moves the animal to that centre from that moment.
  • Foster: foster stays are found exactly as in Phase 1; only the centre and type are added.

Where centre and type come from

ShelterBuddy keeps three histories for an animal. Only two of them record a centre and a type:

On the Edit Animal Details screen, the centre on each status is the Information Only Region field, and the type is Type. Staff set them each time they record a status, so the Status History holds the centre and type at every point of the stay.

Checked across all records: the intake's Physical Location and the status's Region use the same centre ids and names (46 and 47 centres; only Kitimat appears on statuses alone). Animal types also match (Fowl, Goat, Cattle and Sheep appear on statuses only).

Animal type families

ShelterBuddy has about 30 animal types. Several are the same animal at different ages, so we report families. A change inside a family (Kitten → Cat, Puppy → Dog) does not move the animal; a change between families (usually a corrected type) does.

Fostered animals with no care stay at all (0.10%)

Some animals were fostered, so Phase 1 counts them, but they never have a care stay: their only source record has a service type that is not Incoming, such as Found, Boarding or Dispatch Information. Their status history shows they really were in our care (most often In Foster or Available for Adoption - In Foster), so the cause is the source type chosen at intake, not a missing animal.

How the counts read by centre and type

MeasureBy centre and type, for a month or year
Animals in CareDistinct animals in care for at least one day at that centre, in that family
Fostered AnimalsDistinct animals in foster for at least one day while at that centre, in that family
% Animals in Care FosteredFostered Animals ÷ Animals in Care, for the same centre, family and period
Centre figures must never be added together.

An animal transferred during a month was in care at two centres that month. It counts once at each centre, but only once in the province-wide total. Transfers are common and increasing: the sum of all centres is 4.9% above the province total in 2006 and 17.7% above it in 2026. The province total is always its own distinct count. The same applies to families, though a family change is rare (0.04% of stays).

Known limitations

  • The centre is what staff record on each status. If a status is recorded at the wrong centre, the animal is counted there; centre changes without a transfer record are listed for review.
  • Within one day, order matters. An animal can be at two centres on the same day (as in the Animal Protection Services example); it counts at both.
  • Centres change over time. Some centres closed (e.g. Delta, Coquitlam, Nelson) and Kitimat appears only as a transfer destination; centre trends should show the years each centre has data.
  • No grouping of centres. ShelterBuddy has no region above the centre; a centre → region list must come from the data owner.
