Animal types and foster: which types are never fostered?

Version 0.1Draft for SB Data Owner and Foster Manager reviewOctober 2026

Every intake has an animal type. Some types are almost never fostered: by SOP, wild animals are released, sent to a rehabilitation centre or euthanized, not placed in a foster home. Counting them in Intake Animals or Animals in Care lowers the foster rate without telling us anything about foster. This document asks the SB Data Owner: are these animal types never meant to be fostered, and should we filter them out when we measure foster? A suggestion for grouping the remaining types follows as a second step.

The question

6 animal types were fostered less than 1% of the time since 2019. Should they be filtered out of Intake Animals and Animals in Care when measuring foster, and shown separately?
  • They are 17,089 intake animals since 2006 (3.6% of all), of which 102 were ever fostered; since 2019, 3 of 5,179.
  • In 2025, filtering them out changes % Animals in Foster from 31.1% to 32.3%, and % Animals in Care Fostered from 28.7% to 29.7%.
  • Filtered animals are not deleted: they stay in ShelterBuddy and are shown separately in the report.

The next lowest types are fostered far more often (e.g. Farm Animal - Exotic, Bird - Exotic: about 8–14%), so the line between the candidates and the rest is clear.

Why filter by animal type, not only by source type

The source type document already sets aside wildlife, dead-on-arrival and end-of-life source types. But wildlife animals do not always come in through those source types:

Both rules are needed

44.0% of wildlife intakes (11.5% since 2019) came in through an ordinary source type, for example an injured bird brought by the Ambulance or a wild animal picked up as ACO - Impound. Only the animal type shows they are wildlife.
