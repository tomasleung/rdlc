Foster Centre & Animal Type — First Diagnostic Analysis

Draft v0.1 · October 2026 · Intake and Fostered Animals by centre, animal type and time · real data, 2006 – September 2026
First draft for discussion. Fostered Animals are exactly the Phase 1 report; Intake Animals are the Phase 1 intake count. Each fostered animal is given the centre and animal type of its intake (its latest incoming intake before the foster), the same records as the Intake Animal Count, using the raw ShelterBuddy values. Transfers between centres are not counted yet: the Phase 2 preview tab shows what changes with the Phase 2 logic. The rate shown is % Animals in Foster = Fostered Animals ÷ Intake Animals, using all animals of the year (the Phase 1 measure). It is a ratio of two counts, not a share of one group: some fostered animals came in the year before, so a value can even pass 100%. The Phase 2 measure, % Animals in Care Fostered, follows once the definitions are approved. Descriptive only: it shows where things differ, not why.

What we found

Based on 2025, the latest full year (January–December), unless a card says otherwise: cards 1 and 2 compare years (2006–2025, and January–September 2026 with the same months of 2025); card 4 is the average month over 2022–2025; card 6 compares 2019 with 2025. Centre cards cover every active centre (34 with intake in 2025).

1 · VolumeIntake fell far more than fostering.Intake animals are down -65% since 2006; fostered animals -25%. So % Animals in Foster doubled (14.4% → 31.1%). The jump in 2020 (27.2% → 36.5%) is the -30% drop in intake, not more fostering.
2 · Recent trendSince 2022, fostering falls faster than intake.Fostered animals -22% from 2022 to 2025, intake -8%. In 2026 so far both fall at the same pace (-8% and -9%).
3 · Animal typeCats are 62.8% of intake but 72.3% of fostered animals.% Animals in Foster: Cat 35.8%, Dog 24.5% (Rabbit 48.0%). Dog fostering has grown most: 8.9% in 2006.
4 · SeasonCats and dogs peak in opposite seasons.Cat fostering grows 2.9× from February to June–October (kitten season). Dog fostering is lowest in summer (146 a month) and highest in winter (192), while dog intake is flat.
5 · Preview: transfers15.0% of fostered animals are fostered at another centre than their intake.This draft credits the intake centre. With the Phase 2 logic, Vancouver would gain fostered animals and Kamloops, Quesnel, Williams Lake and South Peace would lose some (tab Phase 2 preview).
6 · Intake definitionRichmond's intake grew from 66 to 1,128 animals.In 2025, 350 of them (31%) were dead on arrival (DOA, mostly DOA - ACO). DOA intakes are never fostered (none of the 649 DOA intakes province-wide in 2025), so they lower a centre's rate. Without DOA, Richmond's rate is 19.5%.
7 · Concentration, 2025Ten centres hold 52.7% of fostered animals.Alberni-Clayoquot, Kamloops, West Kootenay, qathet lead. Across the 34 active centres the median rate is 31.5%; rates range from 12.4% to 75.6%, leaving aside Vernon, North Cariboo and Tri-Cities (1, 12 and 24 intake animals), whose intake is too small for a rate to mean much.
8 · Centres: demand and rate, 20258 centres combine high demand with a high foster rate.West Kootenay, qathet and Shuswap take in about 470 animals each and foster about half; Prince Rupert, just under the demand median, fosters 51% of 422. With high demand but a low rate: Maple Ridge, South Peace, Williams Lake and Quesnel. Richmond's 13.5% is partly DOA: without DOA it is 19.5%, still below the median (tab Centre × Type).
9 · Centres: cats and dogs, 2025Most cat-heavy centres foster at high rates (Surrey is the exception); the dog-heavy centres foster few of their dogs.Most cats: Campbell River 92%, Salt Spring Island 90%, Surrey 86%, Sea To Sky 86%, Prince Rupert 82% of intake. Most dogs (25% province-wide): Maple Ridge 42%, Williams Lake 39%, South Peace 38%, West Vancouver 36%, Burnaby 35%. Of their dog intakes they fostered Maple Ridge 31 of 207, Williams Lake 37 of 258, South Peace 19 of 185. Most dogs fostered: Shuswap (69 of 165 dog intakes, 42%); most cats: Alberni-Clayoquot (285 cats).

Which centres foster the most animals, and at what rate?

Key insight
  • Large demand and a high rate: West Kootenay, qathet and Shuswap take in about 470 animals each and foster about half of them (46–54%); Alberni-Clayoquot and Kamloops foster the most animals (317 and 256) at 34–38%.
  • Richmond has the largest intake but a low rate: 1,128 intake animals and 13.5%. 350 of them (31%) were dead on arrival (DOA), which is never fostered. Without DOA the rate is 19.5% (778 intakes), still below the median (31.5%).
  • Large demand and a low rate: Maple Ridge (12%), South Peace (15%), Williams Lake (21%) and Quesnel (23%). Williams Lake and Quesnel send many animals to other centres (see Phase 2 preview), so part of their fostering happens elsewhere.
  • High rate on small demand: Cowichan (76% of 180), Sea To Sky (58% of 104) and Campbell River (44% of 162) are mostly cats; a few animals move their rate a lot. Prince Rupert stands out: 51% on 422 intakes.
  • A rate above 100% (North Cariboo, 125%: 15 fostered, 12 intake) means animals fostered this year that came in the year before; its intake fell from 1,401 in 2024 to 12 in 2025, and 14 of the 15 were fostered at other centres. The Phase 2 measure removes both effects; the drop in intake is a question for the data owner.

Suggested operational improvements

For discussion with the Foster Manager and SB Data Owner
  • Plan foster capacity by season and species: recruit for cats from March, ahead of the kitten season; check whether the summer dog dip is capacity used by kittens.
  • Report by the centre holding the animal, with Animals in Care as the base (Phase 2), so receiving centres such as Vancouver are credited and sending centres are not penalised (see Phase 2 preview).
  • Group animal types into 4 reporting groups (Cat, Dog, Small Animal incl. Rabbit, Other) so every animal is counted.
  • Compare centres within a family (Cat with Cat, Dog with Dog), and show the centre's mix next to its rate.
  • Show DOA intakes separately in centre comparisons: they are never fostered.
  • Close foster location records at the outcome: 1,170 foster placements continue after the animal's care stay ended (SOP reminder).

Why it matters
  • Approving the Phase 2 definitions (Foster Analysis Pack – Phase 2) turns this preview into the report: centres are credited for the animals they hold, and % Animals in Care Fostered replaces % Animals in Foster.
  • Animal type changes the same way (Kitten → Cat) but stays within its family, so the family figures in this draft hardly move.

Method and caveats

  • Fostered Animals: Fabric gold_foster.fact_foster_animal_month, exactly the Phase 1 report: distinct animals in foster for at least one day in the period; stays over 365 days counted as 365.
  • Intake Animals: Fabric gold_foster.vw_fact_intake: distinct animals with an incoming intake dated in the period, at the intake's centre.
  • Centre and animal type of a fostered animal: those of its latest incoming intake on or before the foster start (99.8% of foster-months), else its first intake after, else its service record; raw ShelterBuddy names. Transfers are not counted in this draft (see Phase 2 preview). Families: Cat (Cat, Kitten), Dog (Dog, Puppy), Small Animal, Rabbit, Other.
  • Rate: % Animals in Foster = Fostered Animals ÷ Intake Animals × 100, all animals of the period, the Phase 1 measure. It mixes two populations (in foster during the period vs came in during it) and is distorted by transfers; Phase 2 will use Animals in Care.
  • Counting: an animal counts once per period and group; centre and family rows are not additive.
  • Centre charts: all centres; the type bars (Cat, Dog, Other) only where the centre took in 10+ animals of that type; bars do not add up to 100%. Medians and the demand groups use every active centre (intake in the year); the rate range and the cat and dog shares leave aside centres with fewer than 30 intake animals.
  • Season: average month over 2022–2025. 2026: January–September only; compared with the same months of 2025.
  • Descriptive only: differences are shown, not explained or judged. Pending decisions (Foster Analysis Pack – Phase 2) may change the figures slightly.
